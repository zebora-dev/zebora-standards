## 1. Brand personality

**Smart. Challenging. Credible. Human. A little irreverent.**

RateMyPrompts is the slightly outspoken measurement expert who knows their stuff, questions conventional thinking and doesn't take themselves too seriously.

We're challenging an industry that's investing heavily in GEO measurement without always questioning the quality of the prompts behind it.

We have strong opinions, but we can back them up. We understand complex measurement methodology, but explain it in straightforward language.

And we have a sense of humour.

In a world increasingly full of AI-generated brands, corporate jargon and interchangeable SaaS messaging, RateMyPrompts should feel like it was created by actual people with a point of view.

**Our personality in one sentence:**

_A smart, slightly irreverent GEO measurement nerd who questions everything, knows their stuff and isn't afraid to say what they think._

## 2. Our tone of voice mix

### 50%: The Challenger

Confident, provocative, curious and willing to question established thinking.

We challenge assumptions about GEO measurement, expose weak practices and encourage people to question the numbers they're relying on.

We don't manufacture controversy. We make people think.

**Sounds like:**

- "Your GEO tool is only as good as the prompts behind it."
- "You can measure the wrong thing very accurately."
- "Bad prompts. Bad measurement."

### 30%: The Measurement Expert

Rigorous, knowledgeable, independent-minded and evidence-led.

Our credibility comes from real GEO measurement experience and a structured methodology, not just strong opinions.

We explain the reasoning behind our claims and are transparent about what we can and cannot measure.

**Sounds like:**

- "A good prompt doesn't necessarily make a good measurement framework."
- "Your prompt set determines what your visibility data actually represents."

### 20%: The Straight Talker

Direct, human, approachable and refreshingly uncomplicated.

We make complex methodology understandable without unnecessary technical language or corporate buzzwords.

We communicate like intelligent people talking to other intelligent people.

**Sounds like:**

- "Lots of prompts doesn't mean good prompts."
- "Before you trust the numbers, check the questions."

_These proportions are a creative guide, not a literal writing formula._

## 3. Our seven tone of voice principles

### 1. Challenge assumptions, not people

We question practices, methods and industry conventions. We don't belittle customers, agencies or competitors.

We're comfortable being provocative, but don't confuse provocation with aggression.

### 2. Be confident, not arrogant

We make strong, direct statements when we can support them.

Avoid exaggeration, sweeping claims and pretending we've solved everything.

Where evidence is incomplete or a conclusion is uncertain, we say so.

### 3. Make the complicated simple

We know our subject deeply enough to explain it clearly.

Use accessible language, concrete examples and short, purposeful sentences.

Use technical terminology when it adds meaning, not simply to demonstrate expertise.

We should be accessible without being superficial. Our language is straightforward, but our thinking and methodology need to stand up to scrutiny from experienced GEO, SEO and measurement specialists.

### 4. Be sharp, not gimmicky

We like memorable observations, unexpected phrasing and occasional punchy statements.

But every line must communicate something useful.

Don't sacrifice clarity for a clever headline.

### 5. Have a sense of humour

Dry wit, understated sarcasm, self-awareness and occasional cheekiness are welcome.

The best humour comes from recognising the absurdities and contradictions in GEO measurement.

We don't force jokes, use endless prompt-related puns or try too hard to sound quirky.

### 6. Sound unmistakably human

Our writing should feel natural, conversational and occasionally unexpected.

Vary sentence lengths. Ask questions. Use contractions. Allow a little personality into explanations.

Avoid the overly polished, formulaic and relentlessly upbeat language associated with generic AI-generated content.

**Human doesn't mean sloppy. It means sounding like someone with a brain and a personality wrote it.**

### 7. Be rigorous where it matters

Our methodology, scores, recommendations and technical explanations must be clear, accurate and defensible.

Never exaggerate our capabilities or imply statistical validity that hasn't been established.

The personality can be playful. The measurement must be serious.

## 4. Our sense of humour

**Dry. Intelligent. Occasionally cheeky. Never silly for the sake of it.**

We don't need jokes everywhere.

A well-placed line can make the brand feel more memorable and distinctly human.

**Examples of the right territory:**

- "1,000 prompts. Very impressive. But are they the right 1,000?"
- "Your dashboard looks confident. Should it?"
- "Your prompts have been asking all the questions. Now it's their turn."
- "Turns out, asking the right questions matters. Who knew?"

Humour should be occasional and naturally earned, not a mandatory feature of every paragraph.

Avoid trying to become a comedy brand, relying on memes, or mocking people for poor measurement practices.

## 6. How the tone adapts by context

**Homepage and brand messaging.** Confident, challenging and memorable. Lead with a clear point of view, then explain it simply. Occasional wit is welcome.

**Social media and campaigns.** Our most expressive voice. More room for provocative observations, humour, personality and challenging industry conventions.

**Educational content and thought leadership.** Intelligent, questioning and accessible. Strong opinions supported by evidence and practical examples.

**Product interface and onboarding.** Friendly, concise, direct and useful. Personality should make the experience more engaging, never harder to understand.

**Scorecards, diagnostics and methodology.** Precise, objective, constructive and transparent. Explain why a prompt has been marked down and what the result does or doesn't mean. Avoid humour that undermines the credibility of the assessment.

**Errors and sensitive communications.** Clear, respectful and helpful. No jokes when something has gone wrong, or when users are concerned about their data.

## 7. Language and writing conventions

**We favour:**

- Short, direct sentences mixed with natural conversational explanations.
- Plain English over business jargon.
- Active language over passive descriptions.
- Specific observations over generic claims.
- Questions that make the reader reconsider an assumption.
- British English spelling and natural conversational contractions.
- Occasional punchy standalone lines when they genuinely add emphasis.

**We avoid:**

- Generic SaaS language such as "unlock", "supercharge", "revolutionise", "leverage" and "cutting-edge".
- AI hype and claims that everything is being transformed.
- Excessive exclamation marks, emojis and forced enthusiasm.
- Overly polished marketing copy that could belong to any AI startup.
- Relentless three-part slogans and repetitive rhetorical patterns.
- Claims that our methodology is scientifically proven, independently certified or infallible unless substantiated.
- Treating every low score as proof that a customer's GEO platform is unreliable.

**Technical language has its place.**

We use technical terminology when it genuinely helps explain something, but never just to demonstrate expertise. We don't dumb things down, and we don't make things unnecessarily complicated.

## 8. What makes RateMyPrompts different from Zebora

Both brands share expertise, rigour and an understanding of GEO measurement.

But their personalities should be distinct.

**Zebora** is the experienced strategic consultancy: commercially grounded, analytical, considered and authoritative.

**RateMyPrompts** is the more outspoken product brand: challenging, accessible, occasionally playful and willing to call out questionable measurement practices.

RateMyPrompts can be more direct and irreverent without losing the credibility inherited from Zebora's methodology.

## 9. Our ultimate writing test

Before publishing anything, ask:

1. **Does it make someone think?** Are we saying something useful or challenging an assumption?
2. **Does it sound human?** Would an intelligent, opinionated person actually say this?
3. **Is it clear?** Can the reader understand the point without decoding industry jargon?
4. **Can we back it up?** Are we being accurate and credible, particularly when talking about measurement?
5. **Does it sound like RateMyPrompts?** Or could it have come from any AI SaaS company?

**Our guiding test: Easy to understand. Hard to dismiss.**

## Our tone of voice in one paragraph

RateMyPrompts is a confident, intelligent and slightly irreverent challenger brand built on serious GEO measurement expertise. We question industry conventions, explain complex ideas in straightforward language and aren't afraid to have a little fun along the way. Our humour is dry, our opinions are informed and our approach is rigorous. We want to make people think differently about GEO measurement, without taking ourselves too seriously or compromising the credibility of what we do.

**Personality in the communication. Rigour in the product.**

## House rules for RateMyPrompts

Not part of the tone of voice document. These are standing decisions that apply to every piece of RateMyPrompts writing, collected from the beta launch (October 2026). The `ratemyprompts-voice` skill checks them.

**Mechanics**

- British English spelling (optimise, analyse, behaviour, colour, programme for events).
- No em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma, a colon or brackets.
- No emoji in product copy, emails or community posts. Exclamation marks rarely, and never two together.

**Words we use for the product (match the live site)**

| Use                                                  | Not                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| prompt set, prompts                                  | prompt library, query list                            |
| overall score, scores and assessment, scorecard      | grade, letter grade, rating (in user-facing copy)     |
| our analysts review every set                        | AI-powered analysis, automated audit                  |
| founding places (lead with the total: "50 founding places") | slots, spots, seats                            |
| the waitlist                                         | the queue                                             |
| GEO measurement, AI visibility tracking              | AI SEO hacks, ranking in ChatGPT                      |

**Rules that are not negotiable**

1. **The count rule.** Say "50 founding places". Never say or imply how many are taken or left ("only 12 left", "nearly full", "38 of 50"), and never suggest where someone is in a queue.
2. **Claims register.** Use only figures with a named source, and state the sample. Current figures we can use:
   - 22 brand panels on the Zebora platform, 8,912 prompts, about 28,000 scored answers: unbranded prompts produced mention rates of 0.35% to 1.65%, and prompt styles dominated by branded prompts looked 4 to 20 times better.
   - At a 25% mention rate, 100 prompts carry a margin of about ±8.4 points on one month, and a month-on-month change needs to be around 17 points before you can trust it (standard sampling arithmetic).
   - Anyone else's figure is credited by name in the same sentence (for example Go Fish Digital's wording study).
3. **The scoring is still being finalised.** Do not quote rubric weightings, thresholds or score bands in public copy until the scoring system is confirmed. Talk about what we look at (branded share, buyer language, set size, duplicates, coverage), not the numbers behind it.
4. **Never criticise a named tool or a named customer's set.** Challenge the practice ("prompt lists built from keyword research"), not Peec, Profound, Semrush, Ahrefs, Otterly or Scrunch, and not the person.
5. **Disclose in communities.** Any Reddit, Slack or forum post says who we are in the first line ("I work on RateMyPrompts at Zebora").
6. **Never name Zebora clients** in public copy without written permission.
7. **The 72-hour promise** applies only to confirmed founding places. Waitlist copy makes no time promise.
